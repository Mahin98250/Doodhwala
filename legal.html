Draft for legal review — not a final or effective policy

This page is a working draft prepared from the current app features. It must be reviewed by a qualified Indian legal professional and completed with verified operator, address, grievance contact, retention, seller-verification and operational details before public commercial launch. Do not treat this draft as proof of legal compliance or as an accepted user agreement.

TRUST & TRANSPARENCY

Legal & Privacy

Doodhwala is designed as a marketplace where independent local milk sellers offer products and fulfil customer orders. These draft sections explain the intended rules and data practices. The final terms must match how the live service actually operates.

1. Privacy Policy — draft

Operator identity is incomplete. “DoodhWala” is the operating name supplied for this draft. The exact legal entity or individual operator, registered/contact address and privacy contact have not yet been confirmed. This section must not be treated as final until those details are supplied and reviewed.

Information the app may process

  • Account information such as name, email address, phone number and authentication status.
  • Delivery details supplied by the customer, including recipient name, address, locality, city and PIN code.
  • Precise delivery coordinates when a user chooses to set a map pin or use device location; these can be used to find nearby sellers and support delivery.
  • Order details, selected products, quantities, notes, status history, subscriptions and ratings.
  • Seller profile, product, service-area and order-fulfilment information supplied by independent sellers.
  • Saved favourites and other preferences kept in browser storage.
  • Technical diagnostics or error reports where the app sends them, plus information processed by authentication and hosting services.

Why information is used

The intended purposes are to create and secure accounts, display nearby sellers, route orders to the selected independent seller, manage delivery and subscriptions, prevent fraud or abuse, provide support, troubleshoot errors and meet applicable legal obligations. Information should not be collected for unrelated purposes without an appropriate legal basis and any required notice or consent.

Who may receive information

Information may be processed by the infrastructure needed to operate the service, including Supabase for authentication/database services, GitHub Pages for hosting, and third-party assets or map services used by the website. A seller fulfilling an order needs the relevant order and delivery details. The final policy must describe the actual providers, their roles, applicable terms and any cross-border processing after verification. Do not assume every third party is a data processor or that data stays in India.

Location and browser storage

Location permission is optional for map-based discovery. Users should be able to browse without granting device location where the feature allows it. The site uses browser storage for app functions such as cart or preferences and may use authentication storage to maintain a session. Clearing browser data can remove locally saved items. The final release should verify exactly which keys are stored and provide suitable controls.

Retention, sharing and security

Access should be limited to people and services that need the information. Retention periods have not yet been approved or documented; they must be set by data category, business need and applicable law before launch. Do not promise a fixed deletion time until it is implemented and verified. No sale of personal information is intended by this draft, but the final policy must accurately describe the actual data flows and any disclosures.

Required before launch: confirm the operator's legal identity and address, actual service providers, data retention schedule, privacy contact, deletion/correction workflow and any applicable statutory notices.

2. Marketplace Terms — draft

Doodhwala is intended to help customers discover independent milk sellers and submit orders. Independent sellers, rather than Doodhwala itself, are intended to fulfil the listed milk orders. The final agreement must accurately state whether Doodhwala acts only as a marketplace intermediary or takes on additional roles in pricing, delivery, payments, quality control or fulfilment.

Customer responsibilities

  • Provide accurate contact and delivery information and keep account credentials secure.
  • Review the seller, milk type, quantity, displayed price, delivery details and order status before ordering.
  • Use the service lawfully and not misuse accounts, promotions, ratings or seller contact information.

Independent sellers and listings

Sellers are responsible for the accuracy of information they submit, the products they offer, their fulfilment commitments and their compliance with applicable food-safety, labelling, licensing, tax and consumer-protection requirements. A profile, listing or presence on Doodhwala must not be represented as proof that a seller or product has been independently verified unless a real verification process has been completed and the displayed status supports that claim.

Orders, prices and service availability

Availability, prices and delivery estimates may depend on the seller and service area. The app should clearly show whether an order is pending, accepted, rejected, cancelled or completed, and must not describe an order as accepted or delivered without a reliable status. The final terms must specify the point at which an order becomes binding and how price changes are handled.

Payments, liability and consumer rights

The current checkout interface states that online payment is not enabled and that no payment is collected on that screen. This statement must be updated if payment features change. Nothing in this draft is intended to remove rights that consumers have under applicable law. Responsibility for a complaint, refund, unsafe product, failed delivery or loss must be determined under the actual business arrangement and applicable law; this draft does not attempt to exclude statutory responsibilities.

Suspension and changes

Accounts or listings may need to be restricted for suspected fraud, safety concerns, unlawful activity or material rule violations, using a fair and documented process. Final terms must explain notice, review and how material policy changes are communicated.

3. Cancellation, Refund and Subscription Policy — draft

No specific refund promise, cancellation cutoff, refund processing time or recurring-plan billing rule has been approved for this draft. We must not invent one. The final policy must be completed to match the actual order and subscription system before customers rely on it.

Topics the final policy must answer

  • When customers can cancel an order and whether cancellation depends on seller acceptance or preparation.
  • How to report missing, late, incorrect, damaged or potentially unsafe milk.
  • Who reviews a complaint and how a refund, replacement or other remedy is decided under applicable law.
  • Whether any delivery fee or other charge exists, when it is disclosed and whether it can be refunded.
  • How recurring plans are paused, skipped, resumed or cancelled, including any cutoff time and price changes.
  • How refunds are issued if a payment method is introduced in the future.

Launch blocker: publish an operationally accurate cancellation/refund policy and test each advertised control. Do not promise “instant refunds,” a fixed response time or a specific remedy unless it is approved and can be delivered.

4. Independent Seller Agreement — draft

Independent sellers must provide truthful business and contact details, maintain accurate prices and availability, fulfil accepted orders responsibly and respond to order issues through the process Doodhwala establishes. Sellers must comply with applicable laws and food-safety requirements for their activity and location, including obtaining any licences or registrations that apply to them. Doodhwala must verify which documents are legally required for each seller type before claiming that a seller is compliant.

Seller commitments to finalise

  • Product descriptions, milk type, quantity/unit, price, storage and handling information must be accurate and not misleading.
  • Sellers must follow applicable hygiene, safe handling, transport, packaging, labelling and traceability rules.
  • Sellers must not list products they are not authorised or able to supply.
  • Sellers must protect customer data, use it only for the order or another lawful purpose, and not reuse it for unsolicited marketing.
  • Sellers must cooperate with safety complaints, order investigations and lawful requests, and promptly report material incidents.
  • Verification badges may be shown only after the relevant checks are genuinely completed and kept current.

The final agreement must state commission/fees if any, seller onboarding and suspension rules, recordkeeping, complaint escalation, data-sharing duties, liability allocation and termination procedure. Those commercial terms are not yet supplied and are intentionally omitted.

5. Customer Complaints and Grievance Handling — draft

Customers should have a clear, accessible way to raise complaints about orders, sellers, payments if introduced, unsafe products, privacy, account access or other service problems. A complaint process should create a reference number, record only necessary details, route the complaint to an authorised person, track its status and communicate the outcome.

Support contact: not yet available. No email address, telephone number or designated grievance contact has been supplied. We will not invent one. A real support channel and any legally required grievance-officer details, response timelines and escalation information must be confirmed, published and tested before public commercial launch.

Where an issue concerns immediate food safety, the final process should tell customers how to stop consuming a suspect product and how to reach the appropriate seller or competent authority. The exact wording and escalation contacts require review and verification.

6. Consent and Personal Data Rights — draft

The final service must provide clear, understandable notices at the relevant point of collection and obtain consent or another lawful basis as required. Optional marketing must be separate from essential order processing. Users should have a workable process to request access to information, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent where applicable, grievance redressal and other rights available under applicable law.

Some records may need to be retained where the law permits or requires it, or where necessary to resolve a dispute; the final policy must describe the actual criteria rather than promise that every record can be deleted immediately. The service must also decide how requests are verified and handled when an account cannot sign in.

Do not collect agreement to this draft as if it were final. After legal approval, version the final policies, implement the required acceptance and withdrawal flows, and record acknowledgements securely where required.

7. Security and Responsible Disclosure — draft

Doodhwala's technical baseline uses Supabase authentication, row-level database controls and a public publishable browser key. Publishable keys are not secret credentials; service-role or secret keys must never be placed in browser code or static hosting. Database permissions, row-level security, server-side authorisation, dependency updates and access logs require ongoing review.

Users should never send passwords, one-time codes, payment PINs or secret credentials in a complaint. A public security-reporting contact has not yet been supplied, so a responsible-disclosure address must be established before the policy is finalised.

Security cannot be guaranteed absolutely. If a security incident occurs, the operator must assess the incident, protect affected people, preserve necessary evidence and make any notifications required by applicable law.
